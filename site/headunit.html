Head-Unit SoC Architecture

A modern cockpit SoC runs the instrument cluster, the infotainment system and back-end services side by side. A Type-1 hypervisor splits one chip into isolated virtual machines, so that a crashing app on Android can never freeze the speedometer. This module is the map for the rest of the track.

Analogy

The SoC is an office building. The hypervisor is the building management: it owns the walls, power and the front door, and rents fully separated floors to tenants. The cluster is the safety office on the ground floor with its own emergency exit; Android is the busy open-plan floor; Linux SE is the mail room that talks to the outside world. Tenants talk only through intercoms the management installed.

10.4Boot-time budget

Regulation and user expectations set hard deadlines. The architecture exists largely to meet them: the safety-relevant pieces boot first and never wait for Android.

MilestoneTypical targetOwner
Splash / logo on the cluster< 1 sBootloader or QNX host
Telltales & speed on the cluster< 2 sCluster VM
Rear-view camera after selecting reverse< 2 s (regulatory)Host or cluster (early RVC)
Linux SE network & gateway up< 5 sLinux SE VM
Android home screen usable< 15–25 s (often hidden by suspend-to-RAM)Android VM
